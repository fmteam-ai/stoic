# Fix costs and trend filters before adding AI

The biggest gains available to STOIC are not new AI. They are lower trading costs, a firm rule to trade only with the hourly trend, and stricter testing. Every trade pays a spread and commission, and that cost takes roughly **5-15% of a $2-4.50 gold target**. That is about the size of the small edge most short-term strategies have before costs, which explains why the scalpers are roughly break-even. Your own finding that trades against the H1 trend lost money matches the strongest research available: stop-losses and quick exits only pay when prices keep moving in one direction. The latest AI research from 2025-2026 is sobering. Frontier AI models trading on their own mostly lost money or trailed buy-and-hold, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost about 31% in one live contest. That supports keeping Claude as a news gatekeeper and risk filter, not a trader. The best "AI" addition is a small, carefully tested filter that learns which of the bot's own signals to skip. Pair it with position sizing that adjusts to volatility. Most of the useful data is free or cheap, and several connectors are already attached: Alpha Vantage, FMP and Bigdata.com. Two unused plugins in your directory would help most with testing: Anthropic's official Data plugin and QuanterLab. Every change below should run in shadow first, with pass/fail rules written down before the test starts, because one week of trades is far too few to prove anything.

A note on evidence quality: the research team could search the web but could not open many pages directly. Many figures therefore come from search summaries of the cited pages. Each claim below is labelled strong, mixed or weak. Broker prices in particular should be checked against your own MT5 symbol settings.

## Costs eat 5-15% of every target, which explains break-even scalpers

On "raw" or ECN accounts, gold costs about **$0.09-0.12 per ounce in spread plus about $0.06-0.07 per ounce in commission** during busy hours, roughly $0.15-0.20 per ounce for a round trip. Standard accounts run nearer $0.30 ([chiefidea](https://www.chiefidea.com/forex-brokers/latest/forex-broker-with-the-lowest-xauusd-spread/); [Pepperstone](https://pepperstone.com/en/go/trade-gold-on-razor); [spready.io tracker, Oct 15 2026](https://www.spready.io/spreads/xauusd)). Two of your brokers are covered by third-party figures. VT Markets is listed at about $0.30 spread on Standard and $0.10 on Raw, plus $3 per lot each way in commission ([BrokerAnalysis](https://brokeranalysis.com/broker-review/vt-markets/gold-spread/)). RoboForex's commission figures conflict between sources by a large factor ([BrokerAnalysis](https://brokeranalysis.com/broker-review/roboforex/gold-spread/)). Nothing was found on STARTRADER or OnEquity. Against a $2 target, raw-account costs are about 8-10% of the profit. Against $4.50 they are about 3-4%. A standard account roughly doubles both.

Here is why that matters. "R" means the amount risked on one trade. If the stop is about the same size as the target, costs come to roughly **0.04-0.10R per trade, before slippage**. A careful 2026 replication of a well-known opening-range breakout strategy found a gross edge of about **+0.13R per trade** on Nasdaq futures. After realistic costs, none of five markets was distinguishable from zero, and four of the five were negative ([MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). That is a direct warning for breakout_m15, and it describes the scalpers too: a real but thin edge, consumed by costs. Evidence quality: the cost arithmetic is solid. The broker figures are mixed and need checking.

Costs also rise exactly when gold moves most. Spreads widen in the Asian session and around news ([goldenviperea](https://goldenviperea.com/blog/tools-brokers/broker-spreads-gold/)). Research on currency markets found that banks widen spreads by **more than 30%** in the minute before US data releases. That study was not about gold ([B2Broker citing FCA research](https://b2broker.com/news/how-nfp-affects-trading/)). An average US jobs report (NFP) moves gold about **$3.40-4.00 in 15 minutes**, which is your entire target ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-november-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202501091000)). Academic work confirms that 8:30am New York data releases are the largest scheduled movers, and that good US economic news pushes gold down ([Elder, Miao & Ramchander 2012](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i1p51-65.html)). The pre-news flatten rule is therefore well justified. The open question is only how wide the blackout window should be.

The cheapest improvements fix the plumbing. First, record the actual spread at entry and the gap between the requested price and the price you got on every trade, at all four brokers. Without that, nobody can tell whether a strategy fails on logic or on costs. Second, add a hard spread gate, for example "skip if the spread is more than 1.5x the usual spread for that hour." Third, make sure every account is a raw/ECN type. Fourth, host the bot on a VPS in the broker's data centre, usually London LD4 or New York NY4. Vendors charge about $40 a month and claim around 1 ms latency, though their own pages are inconsistent ([NYCServers](https://newyorkcityservers.com/ld4-forex-vps)). Fifth, use limit orders rather than market orders for range_fade and gold_trend_pulse entries, so those trades stop paying the spread on the way in.

## Trading with the hourly trend is the best-supported change

The strongest academic support for your H1 finding comes from Kaminski and Lo (MIT, peer-reviewed). Their work shows that **stop-loss rules lower expected returns when prices wander randomly, and add value only when prices are trending** ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)). Every STOIC trade has a stop, so STOIC does best in exactly the conditions where trends persist. A related equity study found that the first half-hour's move predicts the last half-hour's, and the effect is strongest on volatile, news-heavy days ([Alpha Architect on Gao et al.](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)). At very short horizons the opposite holds: moves tend to reverse ([CESifo](https://cesifo.org/en/publications/2022/working-paper/persistence-high-frequency-financial-data)). In practice, trend-following engines should only trade with the H1/H4 direction, and range_fade should only trade when the hourly trend is flat. Evidence quality is mixed but consistent. The theory is strong, and the gold-specific intraday proof is missing.

That is why the RoboForex H1-alignment test is the right experiment, but one week is too short to settle it. Detecting a modest improvement takes **roughly 400 or more trades per version** (the research team's own estimate, using the rule that statistical uncertainty shrinks with the square root of the trade count). One week will produce far fewer, so treat it as a check that nothing breaks. The cleaner design runs the rule in shadow on all four brokers: log every signal the H1 filter would have blocked, then compare the blocked trades' results with the allowed ones on the same days.

"Market regime" detectors deserve caution. No rigorous study shows that ADX, Hurst-exponent or hidden-Markov-model filters improve intraday gold trading. Hurst values in gold also shift with the macro backdrop, so any fixed threshold is likely to be overfit ([Revista La Salle 2026](https://revistasinvestigacion.lasalle.mx/index.php/recein/article/download/4448/4459/34220)). A simpler measure carries less risk. Divide current M15 volatility (ATR) by its usual level for that hour of the day. A high ratio together with trend alignment sends signals to breakout and pulse. A low ratio with a flat trend sends them to range_fade. Gold's volatility follows a strong daily rhythm, peaking when major markets open and close, with the tightest spreads while Europe is open. That is solid peer-reviewed evidence, though on 2000-2015 data ([Batten et al. 2017](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/plo/pone00/0174232.html)). The same rhythm argues for setting stops and targets in ATR units rather than fixed dollars, because a $2 move means something very different at 3am than at the New York open.

Two of your current rules need testing. Break-even at +0.5R has no direct evidence for or against it. In theory, moving the stop early on noisy short-term moves increases stop-outs and cuts winners. And because of spread and commission, a "break-even" exit is really a small loss. Test 0.5R, 0.75R and 1R side by side in shadow, and consider setting break-even at entry plus costs. Older studies report gold strength in the Asian session and weakness around the London afternoon price fix, but they rely on pre-2015 data from before the fix was reformed ([CBS thesis](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2014-02-28/gold-fix-study-shows-signs-of-decade-of-bank-manipulation)). Treat them as ideas to re-check on your own data, not as edges.

## AI works as a gatekeeper here, not a fortune-teller

The 2025-2026 record of AI models trading on their own is poor. In Alpha Arena Season 1, six frontier models traded $10,000 each in real crypto. Four lost money: **Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost about 30.8% and GPT-5 lost 62.7%**, while the winner, Qwen3 Max, made about 22% from just 43 trades ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). StockBench tested GPT-5, Claude-4 and others over several months and found that most agents fail to beat buy-and-hold ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209v1)). LiveTradeBench found that the models ranked best on general benchmarks are not the best traders ([arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). A May 2026 study, "The Alpha Illusion," re-ran two popular open-source AI trading agents with real costs. One underperformed buy-and-hold on four of five stocks, and the other's Sharpe ratio turned negative ([alphaxiv 2605.16895](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16895)). The well-known multi-agent frameworks (TradingAgents, ai-hedge-fund, FinRobot) are research projects tested on daily data over short periods. None was tested on gold or on intraday trading ([TradingAgents paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v6)). Evidence quality: strong and consistent. Do not give Claude trading authority.

The news reader is still worthwhile, as long as its job stays defensive. The best AI-news study found that GPT-4 reads the initial market reaction to headlines well. The authors call that reaction "non-tradable" because prices adjust before anyone can act, and the returns fell as more traders adopted the technique ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). Gold reprices on data releases within seconds, while an API news feed plus a Claude call takes seconds to minutes. Claude's realistic jobs are to block trades or flatten positions around surprise events, to spot unscheduled shocks such as geopolitics or tariffs, and to supply a "what kind of day is this" label to other filters. Evidence that AI news sentiment gives a tradable edge in gold specifically is weak, and no study shows it.

The news reader also needs protecting. Researchers have shown that headlines containing hidden HTML text, which humans cannot see but the model reads, can flip an AI's sentiment reading ([arXiv 2601.13082](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13082v1)). In another study, a prompt-injection attack cut one agent's return from 7.81% to 0.89% while it kept trading at a normal pace, so the damage would not show up in activity monitoring ([TradeTrap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02261)). Five protections address this. Strip HTML and hidden characters before Claude sees a headline. Require a strict, code-validated output format. Give the news reader no tools at all. Allow it only to reduce, block or flag trades, never to increase size. Pin the exact Claude model version and re-run a fixed test set of past headlines whenever the model changes.

Among other AI methods, the honest ranking from the research is below.

| Method | Evidence | Fit for STOIC | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position size that shrinks when volatility is high, capped at a fraction of "Kelly" | Strong that it cuts tail losses; strong that it does **not** raise returns per unit of risk for gold and currencies ([Harvey et al. 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)) | High | Do it, for smoother results |
| Testing discipline: walk-forward tests, counting every variant tried, pre-registered shadow tests ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) | Strong | High | Mandatory |
| "Meta-labeling": a small model that learns which of the bot's own signals to skip ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling)) | Weak to mixed; practitioners report it often adds nothing ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14706/why-meta-labeling-is-not-a-silver-bullet/)) | Medium-high | Worth one disciplined trial |
| Off-the-shelf forecasting models (TimesFM, Chronos) for predicting volatility | Mixed but promising ([arXiv 2507.07296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07296v1)) | High (no training needed) | Try against simple ATR |
| Deep learning to predict price direction | Weak after costs; one study's Sharpe fell from 0.11 to -0.46 at tiny costs ([arXiv 2112.08534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.08534)) | Low | Hype for this use |
| Reinforcement-learning trading agents | Weak; reviews find unrealistic cost assumptions throughout ([Meng & Khushi](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jdataj/v4y2019i3p110-d252469.html)) | Very low | Avoid |

Meta-labeling fits STOIC because the engines already choose direction. The filter only decides "take or skip." Possible inputs include session, the volatility ratio, spread, minutes to the next news event, the Daily Bias score and Claude's news label. The filter must stay simple, with a handful of inputs and a basic model, because a few thousand trades cannot support anything bigger. It must also be compared with plain rules such as "skip when the spread is high," which often capture most of the gain.

Three 2026 trends matter. First, **CME's 1-ounce gold futures began trading 24/7 on July 24, 2026**, with about 15,000 contracts traded in the first weekend ([Barchart/PRNewswire](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3484644/traders-drive-strong-demand-in-first-weekend-of-cme-group-s-24-7-gold-futures)). Tokenized gold also trades on weekends and is watched as an early signal for Monday gaps ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/tokenized-gold-weekend-price-discovery-cme-closed?rand=50490)). STOIC is already flat on Fridays, so this is not a risk. It is a Monday-open input: a large weekend move could delay the first trades until spreads settle. Second, brokers are opening up to AI. Interactive Brokers launched an official MCP connector on July 28, 2026 ([IBKR](https://interactivebrokers.ie/en/general/about/mediaRelations/7-28-26.php)), while MT5 has only community-built servers of uneven quality ([gittrend](https://gittrend.io/repo/Qoyyuum/mcp-metatrader5-server)). Third, regulation is folding AI into existing algorithmic-trading rules. The EU AI Act's high-risk deadlines moved to December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). For a private demo-account trader, broker terms remain the real constraint.

## Cheap data and plugins worth adding, ranked by value per dollar

Most of the Daily Bias inputs can be had free or cheaply, upgraded with a few additions. FRED is free and provides the 10-year real yield (series DFII10) and GVZ, the gold "fear gauge" built from options prices ([FRED DFII10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFII10); [FRED GVZCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GVZCLS)). GVZ is a ready-made volatility input for sizing and stops. A "synthetic" DXY can be computed free from the bot's own broker currency prices using the standard index weights, which avoids paying for an ICE feed. The calendar is the real gap. The "surprise" part of the Daily Bias needs actual-versus-forecast values, and Finnhub is blocked while FMP's free tier is too. **FMP Starter at about $22 a month** is the cheapest likely fix, but whether that tier unlocks the calendar is unconfirmed. Trading Economics, at about $149 a month or more, is the upgrade if the bot needs data pushed the moment it is released ([NexusFi on FMP](https://nexusfi.com/d/data-providers/financial-modeling-prep/); [apis.io on Trading Economics](https://apis.io/plans/tradingeconomics/tradingeconomics-plans-pricing/)). CME's FedWatch API, which gives market-implied odds of Fed moves and would sharpen the "Fed tone" input, starts at about $25 a month ([CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/market-data-api/fedwatch-api.html)). COT positioning data is weekly ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/node/128971)) and ETF flows are monthly, so both suit a bias score, not entry signals.

Real volume and order-book data, which STOIC lacks, exist only for COMEX futures, and research finds New York futures lead London spot in setting the gold price ([Hauptfleisch, Putniņš & Lucey](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/41414/4/GoldILS%20JFutMkt%20Forthcoming.pdf)). The cheapest route is Interactive Brokers COMEX depth at about **$11 a month** for non-professionals ([IBKR fees](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/en/index.php?f=37645)). The cleanest developer feed is Databento at **$199 a month** from June 2026 ([Databento](https://databento.com/blog/updates-to-subscription-pricing)). No study shows that COMEX order flow predicts retail CFD gold returns, so this belongs later, after costs and trend filters are fixed. Retail sentiment from IG or Myfxbook is free but has no official API and only weak evidence behind it ([Myfxbook](https://myfxbook.com/community/outlook)). Skip it for now.

## A test plan with pass/fail rules George can hold the team to

The research is clear about method. Most retail backtests fail live because many variants get tried and only the best one is reported ([Bailey et al., PBO](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Every variant tried should be logged. Each test should be decided before it starts: what is measured, for how long, and what counts as success. Where possible, shadow tests should run on the **same live signals** as the current version, comparing "trade with filter" against "trade without filter" over the same period, which removes luck from changing market conditions. The success thresholds below are the research team's proposals, not published standards.

| Order | What to test | How | Pass if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost logging plus spread gate (skip if spread > 1.5x that hour's median) | Live on all 4 brokers; gate in shadow for 4 weeks | Blocked trades show worse net results than allowed trades; you get a real cost-per-trade figure per broker |
| 2 | H1 trend gate on all engines (range_fade only when H1 is flat) | Extend the RoboForex test into a 4-broker shadow log | Over 200+ signals, blocked trades average below zero after costs and allowed trades improve on the current average by about 0.05R or more, without deeper drawdown |
| 3 | Break-even trigger: 0.5R vs 0.75R vs 1R vs entry-plus-costs | Shadow simulation on the same trades | The winning setting beats 0.5R on net R per trade over 200+ trades |
| 4 | Time-of-day volatility ratio routing range_fade vs breakout/pulse | Shadow | Each engine's net R improves in its assigned regime |
| 5 | gold_trend_pulse (already in shadow on VTMarkets) | Keep in shadow until 200+ signals | Positive average net R after real VTMarkets costs, with a profit factor above about 1.2 and stable results across months |
| 6 | Daily Bias score as a filter, plus FRED GVZ and real yields | Shadow | Trades aligned with the bias beat misaligned ones over 200+ trades |
| 7 | Volatility-scaled sizing (risk per trade shrinks when ATR or GVZ is high) | Shadow on the same trades | Lower worst-week loss and drawdown with similar total net R |
| 8 | Meta-label filter (only after items 1-7 have produced clean logs) | Walk-forward tests, then shadow | Beats the simple rule filters on net R per trade, not just win rate |

Each run should also be compared against a breakout_m15 baseline net of real costs, given the opening-range warning. If its net average stays near zero after the cost log is in place, retire it, as you did with BTC.

## Conclusion

STOIC has more robust safety rails than most retail bots: daily and weekly breakers, Friday flat, news flattening and one position across accounts. Its weakness is measurement. Until real spread and slippage are logged per broker and every rule change is tested against the same signals, the team cannot tell a good strategy squeezed by costs from a bad strategy. That matters for the scalpers and breakout_m15 in particular. The research also changes what "AI improvement" should mean for this bot. The frontier is not a smarter model choosing trades. Benchmarks show model choice matters less than risk design, and that even the best models lose money trading alone. The gains come from AI that reads news defensively, flags bad conditions, and helps the team run honest statistics on its own trade history.

The practical implication for George: the next three months should yield fewer trades of higher quality, not more engines. The two cheapest steps carry the most value. A cost log with a spread gate costs nothing to add, and the H1 trend gate already has supporting evidence from your own losses. If those lift the scalpers clearly above break-even after costs, the extra data and the meta-label filter become worth their price. If they do not, that answer is useful too: those engines have no edge left once costs are paid, and the account would be better served by gold_trend_pulse and the trend-aligned strategies alone.
