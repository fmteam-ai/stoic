# Gold (XAUUSD) Intraday Edges, Regime Detection and Execution Quality for a Retail MT5 Bot (2025-2026)

Research note on method: the web search tool worked, but direct page fetches failed with DNS errors for nearly every domain tried (icmarkets.com, pepperstone.com, ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, spready.io, mql5.com, investing.com). Most findings below therefore come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full text. Exact figures should be checked against the primary page before anyone relies on them. Evidence-quality labels: [PEER] = peer-reviewed journal; [WP] = working paper or thesis; [BROKER] = broker's own marketing or spec page; [AFFIL] = affiliate or comparison site; [BLOG] = practitioner blog or forum; [VENDOR] = VPS or software vendor.

## Q1. Documented intraday gold patterns: sessions, London fix, US data, time-of-day volatility, momentum vs mean reversion

### Takeaway
The best-supported intraday gold facts are about when the market is active, not which way it moves. Volatility, volume and spreads follow the opening and closing of the world's major markets. The 8:30 ET US data releases (especially NFP) are the biggest scheduled movers, and good US economic news pushes gold down. Evidence for tradeable direction is weaker and mostly older. It includes a "hat-shaped" pattern (Asian-session strength, weakness later in the day) and a downward drift around the London PM fix during the years of alleged manipulation. Intraday momentum, where the first half-hour predicts the last half-hour, is documented for commodity futures in general, but I found no clean gold-specific confirmation. At very high frequencies, persistence tends to fall, which points toward mean reversion over very short horizons.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER, older: 2000-2015 data] Batten, Lucey, McGroarty, Peat & Urquhart (2017, PLoS ONE) used 5-minute data for gold, silver, platinum and palladium from 2000 to 2015. They found strong intraday periodicity in returns, volatility, volume and bid-ask spread. Returns and volume follow the opening and closing of the major world markets, and spreads are tightest while European markets are open. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/plo/pone00/0174232.html); [Reading repository PDF](https://reading-clone.eprints-hosting.org/79175/1/BattenLuceyMcGroartyPeatUrquhart2017.pdf)
- [PEER] The same paper found bilateral Granger causality between returns and volatility for each metal, holding in most subsamples. In other words, volatility clustering and return dynamics interact. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/plo/pone00/0174232.html)
- [PEER] The same paper reported that trade counts rose and bid-ask spreads narrowed over 2000-2015, which the authors read as greater liquidity and efficiency. Rising efficiency usually erodes simple intraday anomalies. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/plo/pone00/0174232.html)
- [PEER, older] Batten & Lucey studied CBOT gold futures (1999-2005) and found significant variation in volatility across the trading day and week, consistent with microstructure theory. Other studies show a U-shaped volatility pattern, with elevated volatility at the open and close. — [Macquarie](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/volatility-in-the-gold-futures-market/); [TCD IIIS DP225](https://www.tcd.ie/triss/assets/PDFs/iiis/iiisdp225.pdf)
- [WP] Iwatsubo, Watkins & Xu (Kobe University) studied intraday seasonality in efficiency, liquidity, volatility and volume for gold and platinum futures in Tokyo and New York (COMEX). — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/koe/wpaper/1722.html); [Kobe PDF](https://www.econ.kobe-u.ac.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/1715.pdf)
- [WP, student thesis] Donati & Jung (CBS master's thesis) report a "hat-shaped" intraday pattern: gold tends to rise during Asian hours and fall for the rest of the day. They also find price drops around the London fixing times in the years of alleged manipulation. The authors attribute the pattern partly to large gold imports by eastern countries and partly to fix manipulation. — [CBS research portal](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/)
- [WP, 2014 draft, not peer-reviewed at the time] Abrantes-Metz & Metz (2014) found unusual trading patterns around the 3pm London PM fix starting in 2004, with price moves tending to be downward. They described this as consistent with price artificiality. The fix was then set on a private call among five dealer banks; it was later replaced by the LBMA electronic auction. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2014-02-28/gold-fix-study-shows-signs-of-decade-of-bank-manipulation); [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/business/2014-03-03-gold-fix-study-indicates-a-decade-of-manipulation/)
- [PEER] Caminschi & Heaney (UWA) found the London PM fixing had a material effect on exchange-traded gold instruments, with evidence that fixing information leaked before publication. The opening minutes of the fixing predicted the fixing's direction, in some cases more than 90% of the time. — [UWA PDF](https://api.research-repository.uwa.edu.au/ws/files/4725264/A0205_use_this.pdf)
- [PEER, older: 2002-2008 data] Elder, Miao & Ramchander (2012, Journal of Banking & Finance) found that metal futures react quickly and significantly to US macro surprises. The 8:30am releases, especially nonfarm payrolls and durable goods, have the largest impact. Unexpectedly good economic news tends to lower gold and silver prices, and macro news raises realized volatility and volume. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i1p51-65.html)
- [PEER, older: 2001] Among 23 US announcements, employment reports, GDP, CPI and personal income had the greatest impact on COMEX gold futures. — [Journal of Futures Markets via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v21y2001i3p257-278.html)
- [BLOG/media, small sample] An FXStreet study of about 35 NFP releases found that 15 minutes after the release, gold rose about $3.97 on average after a payrolls miss and fell about $3.37 after a beat. The effect weakened slightly by four hours, and gold reacts more strongly to disappointing reports. These moves are the same size as the bot's entire $2-4.50 target. — [FXStreet 2025](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-november-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202501091000); [FXStreet Apr 2025](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-april-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202505011030)
- [BLOG/media] During the 2022-23 Fed hiking cycle, 7 of 12 CPI releases moved gold by 2% or more. The direction was regime-dependent: hot CPI could push gold down through rate-hike expectations (-4.5% on one hot print, +4.0% on one soft print). — [BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-trading-price-013120234)
- [PEER, equities, older] Gao, Han, Li & Zhou documented intraday momentum: the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return (SPY, 1993-2013). The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume and macro-news days. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- [WP] One working paper finds the first half-hour return significantly predicts the last half-hour return across four commodity futures markets. The search summary did not confirm that gold was one of them. — [AUT ACFR PDF (Zhou)](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/686816/4a-Z-Ivy-Zhou.pdf)
- [PEER] In Chinese metal futures (gold, silver, aluminum, copper, 1-minute data), the predictive power of the day session's first half-hour disappeared after night trading launched, and the night session's first half-hour became the predictor. Intraday momentum is therefore session- and market-structure-dependent. — [Hull repository](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/3303156); [Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 2023 via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v80y2023ics0927538x2300152x.html)
- [PEER, 2023] Liu, Lu, Li & Wang (2023, Journal of Empirical Finance) found that intraday realized semivariance predicts time-series momentum reversals in commodity futures. A rule-based filter improved the out-of-sample Sharpe ratio after transaction costs. This is a multi-day horizon, and I did not confirm that gold was in the sample. — [Reading repository](https://reading-clone.eprints-hosting.org/111035)
- [WP, equities] A CESifo paper on EuroStoxx 50 futures found that persistence falls as data frequency rises, and intraday returns are anti-persistent, which favors reversal at that frequency. This is not a gold study. — [CESifo](https://cesifo.org/en/publications/2022/working-paper/persistence-high-frequency-financial-data)
- [WGC, 2026] Gold trading volume averaged about $361bn/day in 2025: about $180bn OTC (mostly spot), $174bn in futures and $7bn in ETFs. That is up from about $163bn/day in 2023, with futures up from $62bn to $174bn. — [WGC Goldhub, Liquidity](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-strategic-asset-2026-edition/20449)
- [Retail, unverified] The COMEX pit session is commonly referenced as 08:20-13:30 New York time, with peak liquidity in the London-New York overlap. Sources disagree on when the overlap ends (11:00 or 12:00 ET). — [hw.online](https://hw.online/faq/gold-trading-hours-usa-in-depth-review-market-sessions/)

### Inferences
- In the absence of strong direction evidence, a time-of-day filter is mainly a volatility and cost filter. Trade during the London and London-NY overlap windows, when spreads are tightest and the range comfortably exceeds the $2-4.50 target. Avoid the Asian hours and the 17:00 ET rollover, when spreads widen.
- A typical NFP or CPI move ($3-4 in 15 minutes for NFP; 2%+ daily for CPI in 2022-23) is as large as the whole scalping target. A news blackout around 8:30 ET releases, FOMC and CPI is justified on both cost grounds and direction-randomness grounds.
- The documented direction anomalies (Asian strength, the fix drift) come from pre-2015 data, and the fix was restructured in 2015. Treat them as hypotheses to re-test on the bot's own broker data, not as edges to trade.
- Intraday momentum on news-heavy, high-volume days fits the owner's experience that trend-aligned trades work and counter-trend trades lose. Momentum appears to dominate at multi-hour horizons on active days, with mean reversion confined to very short horizons or quiet ranges.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed 2023-2026 study of XAUUSD intraday seasonality or session returns. Most academic evidence predates 2016.
- I found no gold-specific test of first half-hour / last half-hour momentum or of VWAP-reversion edges.
- I found no post-2015 study of price behavior around the LBMA Gold Price auction (10:30 and 15:00 London) after its move to an electronic auction.
- I found no quantified trend-day vs range-day frequency statistics for gold.

## Q2. Regime detection methods (ADX/ATR ratios, Hurst, HMM, volatility clustering, opening range) and evidence they improve results

### Takeaway
Almost all regime-filter evidence for intraday gold is retail or vendor material. I found no rigorous out-of-sample study showing that ADX, Hurst or HMM filters improve intraday gold scalping. Academic support exists for the general premises: volatility clusters, returns and volatility Granger-cause each other, and Hurst persistence in gold is state-dependent and drops at higher frequencies. Opening-range breakout, the closest analogue to the bot's Donchian engine, reproduces gross but goes to zero after costs in a careful 2026 replication on index futures.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER] Returns and volatility in gold show bilateral Granger causality, and volatility shows strong intraday periodicity. This supports volatility-based regime features, for example ATR measured relative to the same time of day. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/plo/pone00/0174232.html)
- [Preprint] A fractal (Hurst and MF-DFA) study of gold volatility finds patterns that are persistent, anti-persistent or state-dependent depending on macro conditions. — [Research Square](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-7994054/v1/4be3ad16ff22b4d698551fb9.html)
- [PEER, 2026] A study of gold futures found Hurst exponents showed more persistence before the 2022 war and lower values after it. It also found that corrected R/S estimators are more robust than naive ones, so estimator choice matters if Hurst is used as a filter. — [Revista La Salle](https://revistasinvestigacion.lasalle.mx/index.php/recein/article/download/4448/4459/34220)
- [WP, equities] Higher sampling frequency leads to lower persistence, and intraday returns are anti-persistent. — [CESifo](https://cesifo.org/en/publications/2022/working-paper/persistence-high-frequency-financial-data)
- [BLOG/research platform, daily] A QuantConnect study used an HMM fitted to SPY drawdowns to allocate between GLD and SPY. It reported a Sharpe of 0.823, a CAR of 19.8% and a max drawdown of 27.1% over 6 years. This is a daily allocation backtest, not intraday gold trading. — [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18811/optimizing-a-gold-spy-portfolio-using-hidden-markov-models-for-market-downtime/)
- [WP, 2026] A three-state Gaussian HMM on daily data (2004-2025), with states selected by BIC, allocated across SPY, TLT and GLD with reinforcement learning. It is daily, not intraday. — [EconPapers/arXiv](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/arxpapers/2605.27848.htm)
- [WP, older 2013] A coupled HMM used gold dynamics as a regime filter for USDCHF trades, with RSI and CCI as triggers. — [arXiv 1308.0900](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.0900)
- [BLOG, 2026, careful replication] An independent replication of the Zarattini & Aziz opening-range breakout on NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX and FTSE reproduced the gross edge (about +0.13R per trade on NQ). After realistic costs, none of the five markets was distinguishable from zero, and four of five were negative. The original paper charged no spread or slippage and had no out-of-sample period. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [BLOG, 2026] A London-open breakout on gold, backtested over 3.7 years on real-tick data, reported a profit factor of about 1.4. Its 2026 out-of-sample sample was only 157 trades. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540) (could not fetch; summary only)
- [PEER, 2014] Kaminski & Lo found that stop-loss rules reduce expected return under a random walk but add value when there is momentum. They explain this with a regime-switching ("flight-to-quality") model. In practice: stops and trend-following exits only pay in persistent regimes. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-value "regime filter" is probably the one the owner's data already reveals: H1/H4 trend alignment, which matches the momentum-regime logic in Kaminski & Lo. Next would be a time-of-day-normalized ATR filter, for example current M15 ATR divided by the median ATR for that hour over the last N days, to separate trend days from range days. Route range-fade only to low-ratio, flat-trend regimes and breakouts only to high-ratio, trend-aligned regimes.
- Hurst and HMM add estimation noise and parameter risk at M5/M15. Academic results show Hurst in gold shifts with macro state, so a fixed threshold is likely to be overfit. If used, require walk-forward validation.
- The ORB replication is a direct warning for the M15 Donchian breakout engine. Gross breakout edges of about 0.1R are of the same order as retail costs (see Q3), so the engine's edge must be measured net of real spread plus slippage.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or out-of-sample evidence specifically showing that ADX, ADX/ATR ratios, Hurst or HMM filters improve intraday XAUUSD results.
- I found no gold-specific ORB study with costs included.

## Q3. Execution costs on retail gold CFDs: spreads, commissions, news slippage, cost vs a $2-4.50 target, latency/VPS, last look

### Takeaway
On raw/ECN accounts, typical liquid-session XAUUSD costs are about $0.09-0.12/oz spread plus about $0.06-0.07/oz commission, so roughly $0.15-0.20/oz round trip. That is about 3-10% of a $2-4.50 target. Standard accounts are often around $0.30/oz or more, roughly 7-15%. Real costs are higher in the Asian session and around news, and slippage on stops is not counted in these figures. For a strategy that wins about half the time with roughly 1:1 R, costs of about 0.05-0.1R per trade are enough to turn a small gross edge into break-even. That matches the owner's result that the scalpers are about break-even.

### Inferences
- Cost arithmetic (my calculation from the figures above): raw spread $0.09-0.12 plus $0.06-0.07/oz commission gives about $0.15-0.19/oz round trip. Against a $2.00 target that is about 8-10%; against $4.50 it is about 3-4%. A Standard account at about $0.30 gives roughly 7-15%. If the stop is about the same size as the target, costs are about 0.04-0.10R per trade before slippage. That is the same order as the about 0.1R gross edges seen in breakout research, which explains break-even scalpers.
- The highest-leverage execution fixes are probably: (1) use a raw/ECN account; (2) log the actual spread at entry and real fill vs. requested price per trade, then add a hard max-spread gate, for example skip if spread is above 1.5x the hour's median; (3) place the VPS in the broker's data center, usually LD4 or NY4, which is cheap; (4) use limit entries for fade and pullback engines to avoid paying the spread on entry; (5) black out roughly 2 minutes before to 5-15 minutes after tier-1 releases.
- Requotes and last look: MT5 brokers on market execution generally do not requote but slip. "Instant execution" accounts can requote. I found no measured data on last-look rejection rates for retail gold CFDs.

- I found no independent, measured XAUUSD slippage data around NFP/CPI/FOMC in dollar terms, and no independent latency-vs-slippage benchmarks for retail MT5.
- I found no data on last-look rejection rates for retail gold.

## Q4. Practical lessons: trade with the higher-timeframe trend, avoid news, ATR-based stops, breakeven rules

### Takeaway
The available evidence supports trading with the trend in momentum regimes and avoiding news-release windows. Rigorous evidence for specific ATR stop multiples or breakeven rules is thin. Kaminski & Lo show stops only add value when momentum is present, and costs and news slippage hurt tight stops most.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER, 2014] Under a random walk, stop-loss rules lower expected return. They add value only in the presence of momentum, or regime-switching with flights to quality. In daily futures, some stop policies raised expected return while substantially cutting volatility. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- [WP, student, older] Trailing and traditional stops on OMX30 stocks were consistent with Kaminski & Lo's finding of positive marginal impact. This is weak evidence. — [Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/1474565)
- [PEER] Intraday momentum (first half-hour predicting the last half-hour) is stronger on volatile, high-volume and macro-news days. — [Alpha Architect summary of Gao et al.](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- [PEER] US macro news raises gold's realized volatility and volume, with 8:30 ET releases having the largest effect. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i1p51-65.html)
- [BLOG/media] Average 15-minute NFP reactions of about $3.4-4.0 equal the bot's target size. CPI moved gold 2% or more in 7 of 12 releases during 2022-23, with direction depending on the Fed regime. — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-november-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202501091000); [BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-trading-price-013120234)
- [BLOG, 2026] The careful ORB replication shows that ignoring spread and slippage creates spurious edges. Backtests must include realistic costs and out-of-sample periods. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)

### Inferences
- The owner's finding that counter-H1-trend trades lost money fits the momentum and stop-loss theory. The most defensible change is a hard H1 trend-alignment gate on all engines, with range-fade allowed only when the H1 trend is flat or weak.
- ATR-based stops: with intraday volatility strongly time-of-day dependent, fixed-dollar stops are too tight in the London/NY overlap and too loose in Asia. A stop of k × ATR(M5/M15), with the target expressed in the same ATR units, keeps cost-to-R consistent. Choose k by walk-forward testing. I found no study that supports a particular value of k for gold.
- Breakeven rules: there is no direct evidence. In theory, moving to breakeven too early on a noisy, mean-reverting short horizon raises the stop-out rate and cuts winners, and spread plus commission means a "breakeven" exit is actually a small loss. Test breakeven triggers at fractions of R (for example 0.5R, 0.75R, 1R) net of costs before adopting one.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or rigorous out-of-sample study of breakeven-stop rules or optimal ATR stop multiples for gold intraday.
- I found no quantified evidence for gold on how much a news blackout improves net performance. The recommendation rests on the cost and volatility evidence above.
